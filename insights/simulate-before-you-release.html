SYNTRIQ INSIGHTS / SIMULATION & EVIDENCE

Explore change before it reaches operations

Use synthetic scenarios to explore normal operations, difficult exceptions and the effects of a change.

A new business rule may look simple in a specification. Its effects become clearer when a late event arrives, a request is repeated or a correction changes yesterday’s result. Simulation brings those situations into the engineering conversation.

Make scenarios part of the application.

Syntriq’s engineering approach places scenario definitions alongside the models and workflows they exercise. A scenario describes the starting conditions, the sequence of events and the expected outcome. Synthetic data provides the records needed to explore that behaviour without requiring live customer data.

A useful collection contains ordinary journeys, boundary cases and deliberate faults. A repeated submission should not create duplicate effects. An invalid change should leave accepted state intact. A correction should preserve a trace of what changed.

Repeat the experiment.

Repeatable generators and a controlled clock make a failure easier to investigate. An engineer can replay the same inputs against the baseline and a candidate change, then compare the results. The comparison is meaningful because the experiment holds its starting conditions steady.

In ChargeWeave, charging and settlement scenarios provide concrete business examples. Other applications bring their own populations, events and expected outcomes to the same testing approach.

A useful experiment connects an input, an expected result and the evidence of what actually happened.

Bring feedback into the browser.

The browser workbench brings the candidate model, application preview and supported scenario checks into one engineering flow. Teams can inspect a generated form, exercise an action and follow the resulting workflow without treating every experiment as a release.

AI assistance fits into this loop by examining actual failures and proposing bounded changes. The scenario runner remains responsible for execution. Expectations come from requirements and independent checks, rather than from the confidence of an explanation.

Keep evidence attached to the change.

A passing check belongs to a particular candidate and a particular scope. The evidence records the inputs, source version, outcome and relevant trace. If the candidate changes, affected evidence needs to be refreshed.

This gives reviewers a more useful question than “did the tests pass?” They can ask which journeys were exercised, what failed, which assumptions were used and which checks require an external provider.

Connect fast learning to independent verification.

Local feedback supports rapid exploration. Release verification checks the selected source revision independently, including builds and checks that run outside the page. Both contribute to the evidence associated with the change.

Synthetic results explain behaviour under stated assumptions. Real integrations, operational constraints and representative data still require their own evaluation. Keeping the experiment’s scope visible makes its findings more useful.

THE BUSINESS VALUE

Earlier insight into the consequences of change, repeatable investigations and a clearer evidence trail for review.
