Two reasonable reports can disagree

A January report is produced at the end of the month. In February, a correction arrives that changes a fact effective in January. A later report for January now shows a different result. Both reports may be internally consistent, but they answer different questions.

Syntriq’s temporal architecture distinguishes effective time from recorded knowledge. Effective time describes when a fact applies in the business. Recorded knowledge describes when that information became available to the system.

Ask the question with its time perspective

One question is: what do we now understand about January? Another is: what did we understand when the January report was produced? A useful interface makes the distinction explicit through period and knowledge-cutoff context.

That context needs to follow relationships as well as individual values. If responsibility for an agreement changes, the historical relationship can matter to both a decision and a report. Looking only at the current owner may answer the wrong question.

Follow a correction through the views

Imagine an agreement effective from 1 January and recorded on 3 January. A report is generated with a 31 January knowledge cutoff. On 12 February, corrected information arrives that applies from the agreement’s original effective date.

The original-knowledge view retains the basis available at the January cutoff. The corrected view uses the later information. A comparison should show the changed fact and its consequences, giving readers an explanation instead of an unexplained restatement.

Carry time into decisions and measures

A policy evaluation needs to identify the applicable rules and facts. An analytical measure needs a period, an aggregation definition and a knowledge perspective. These contexts should be included in evidence so that later review can reconstruct the question being answered.

Temporal design also requires explicit treatment of corrections, interval boundaries and invalid ranges. Those details belong in supported contracts and scenario tests rather than being left to individual screens.

Give AI the right historical context

An AI assistant investigating a discrepancy should retrieve the relevant versions and cutoff information. Current definitions alone may not explain a historical outcome. The Context Engine should make the selected time perspective visible alongside source provenance.

ChargeWeave offers concrete examples through corrections and operational history. The generic Syntriq story is broader: organisations can explain changing knowledge without erasing the context of earlier decisions.
