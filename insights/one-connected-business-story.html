The cost of disconnected definitions

A customer appears in an onboarding form, an agreement, a workflow and a report. Each view serves a different purpose, but all describe the same business relationship. When those definitions drift apart, people reconcile the differences manually. A changed approval condition can reach the form before it reaches the process. A report can count agreements differently from the operational screen.

Syntriq organises these concerns around a shared application model. The model gives entities and relationships explicit meaning, while contracts describe the behaviour attached to them. The useful connection is between what the business means and what the application does.

Give each layer a clear responsibility

The ontology provides the semantic foundation. Constraints describe acceptable data. Decision policies apply business choices. Workflows coordinate steps, and commands apply permitted changes. These responsibilities connect without becoming interchangeable.

GraphQL offers typed access to the application and its metadata. It is an interface over the model. A query can expose a customer alongside the relationships, constraints or temporal context needed to interpret that customer correctly.

Follow an agreement through the system

Consider an agreement that needs an additional approval when its value crosses a threshold. The form gathers the relevant facts. A decision evaluates those facts against a policy. The workflow routes the agreement to an authorised reviewer. A command records the permitted transition.

The analytical view can then distinguish submitted, approved and activated agreements using the same business definitions. Historical context explains which policy and facts applied when a particular agreement was evaluated.

Give engineering the same context

An engineer changing the approval process needs to see the connected definitions. The application catalogue and Context Engine give AI-assisted engineering a route to the relevant sources, dependencies and checks. A proposed change can be explored in a candidate workspace and exercised against synthetic scenarios.

This creates a common thread between product design and engineering review. The business rule being discussed is tied to the rule being evaluated, and the evidence can refer to the version being reviewed.

Carry the foundation across applications

ChargeWeave provides an EV-domain example: charging relationships, settlement policies, operational workflows and measures form an application package. Syntriq supplies the reusable mechanisms that interpret and execute supported contracts.

Keeping domain definitions separate gives the platform a route into other applications without adding charging-specific branches to the core. Each domain brings its own meaning; the shared foundation preserves the connections.
